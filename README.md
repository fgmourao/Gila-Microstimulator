# 🦎 Gila Monster 
### Constant-Current Stimulator v1.0 

> **Under development**

An electrical microstimulator for constant-current biphasic (or monophasic) stimulation. Pulse timing is generated by the firmware running on an Arduino Uno (Pulse_generator_bifasic.ino).

The hardware is based on and adapted from the [Open-Stim](https://github.com/Open-Stim/openstim) project (UFSJ / LINNCE). In the original architecture, four Arduino outputs (Timer4) time the pulses with 10 µs resolution, and the analog circuit combines these signals into a controlled current. In the present design, only two outputs are used: the firmware directly generates the two stimulation phases, Phase A (D7) and Phase B (D8), with their edges positioned in software. Built on the [Gila-Pulse-Generator](https://github.com/fgmourao/Gila-Pulse-Generator), this version adds a second output port (D8). Every pulse of the selected engine becomes a pair: port A rises, Pulse, port A falls, Delay, port B rises, Pulse, port B falls. The two pulses never overlap, and the delay between them is programmable from 10 µs upwards. All three stimulation modes, Continuous, Burst and NPS, and both Manual and Trigger operation are preserved.<br/>


## Main Modifications

- **User interface:** The Nextion touchscreen interface (Arduino Leonardo) was replaced with a 16×2 I2C LCD and rotary encoder (Arduino Uno), reducing power consumption and simplifying assembly.

- **Battery monitor:** The LM741/zener/LED battery-monitoring circuit was removed to reduce battery consumption.

- **Amplifiers:** TL072 op-amps were replaced with OPA2197 devices, providing rail-to-rail operation, low offset, and operation up to 36 V. This makes better use of the available supply voltage and increases the output compliance range.

- **Simplified signal chain:** The two inverting summing amplifiers (four Timer4 signals) were replaced by a single differential amplifier (A − B) followed by a buffer.

- **Current adjustment:** The resistor board was replaced by a 10-turn potentiometer with a dial and range selector (1 mA, 100 µA, and 10 µA), allowing direct current adjustment and readout.

- **Power supply:** ±18 V from four 9 V batteries (one 1N4007 per rail). The voltage reference and the Arduino each have their own 9 V battery.

- **Isolation:** A digital isolator (ISO7720F) separates the Arduino from the stimulation circuit. Its output side is powered by a REF5050 reference, which fixes the pulse amplitude at 5V.

- **Output protection:** A DC-blocking capacitor (CBLK, 66 µF ceramic) and "bleeder" resistor (RBLEED, 10 MΩ) at the output, pull-down resistors on the inputs, and a passive current limiter (two 1N5304 current-regulating diodes, ~1.8 mA).

- **Output enable:** PhotoMOS relays controlled by D9 connect the electrodes only during stimulation and short them at rest.

- **Compliance alarm:** Two LEDs (one per phase) light when the load exceeds the compliance limit and the current falls below the setpoint.


## Specifications

Values below come from circuit simulation.

- **Output current:** Up to 1 mA, with selectable ranges of 1 mA, 100 µA, and 10 µA. Use the lowest range that covers the desired current (1 mA: 100 µA–1 mA; 100 µA: 10–100 µA; 10 µA: below 10 µA).

- **Maximum load at 1 mA:** Approximately 16 kΩ with a ±18 V battery supply. A working load of up to 12 kΩ is recommended.

- **Compliance voltage:** Approximately 16 V with a ±18 V battery supply, including the ~1 V drop of the current limiter.

- **Battery-dependent compliance:** Maximum load follows approximately Load_max = Compliance / Current. At 1 mA, every 1 V decrease in compliance reduces the maximum load by approximately 1 kΩ. With each rail decreasing from approximately 19 V to 14 V over battery life, the corresponding maximum load is approximately 17 kΩ to 12 kΩ.

- **Frequency:** 0.10–500 Hz (firmware-limited).

- **Minimum phase duration:** 100 µs in Continuous and Burst modes; 50 µs in NPS mode (firmware-limited).

- **Maximum phase duration:** Firmware-limited, capped at 999.99 ms in all modes.
    - Biphasic: (Period − 100 µs − Delay) / 2
    - Monophasic: Period − 100 µs
    - Hardware limit: CBLK charges as I × t / C during each phase. At 1 mA into 10 kΩ, the maximum phase is approximately 460 ms.

- **Hardware limitation, charge accumulation (monophasic only):** In monophasic mode, all charge flows in the same direction and accumulates over the pulse train; RBLEED keeps it bounded. The steady-state voltage across CBLK is approximately I × duty cycle × RBLEED, reached with a time constant of RBLEED × CBLK (~11 min). At 1 mA, 100 µs phases and 4 Hz, this settles around 4 V, which must be subtracted from the available compliance. If I × duty cycle × RBLEED exceeds the compliance margin, the output saturates before steady state is reached. In balanced biphasic mode this does not occur. For a 4 µs mismatch at 1 mA and 4 Hz, the residual is about 0.16 V.

- **Long monophasic protocols:** If the protocol requires net charge, an optional jumper can short CBLK, making the output DC-coupled. This removes the DC protection, and the current limiter and output-enable stage remain the only safeguards.
