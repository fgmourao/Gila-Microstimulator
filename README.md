# 🦎 Gila Monster 
### Constant-Current Biphasic (or Monophasic) Stimulator v1.0 

> **Under development**

An electrical microstimulator for constant-current biphasic (or monophasic) stimulation. Pulse timing is generated by the firmware running on an Arduino Uno (Pulse_generator_bifasic.ino).

The hardware is based on and adapted from the [Open-Stim](https://github.com/Open-Stim/openstim) project (UFSJ / LINNCE). In the original architecture, a microcontroller generates pulses with programmable frequency and timing, and the analog circuit converts these signals into a controlled current. In the present design, the firmware directly generates the two stimulation phases, Phase A (D7) and Phase B (D8), with their edges positioned in software. Built on the [Gila-Pulse-Generator](https://github.com/fgmourao/Gila-Pulse-Generator) , this version adds a second output port (D8). Every pulse of the selected engine becomes a pair: port A rises, Pulse, port A falls, Delay, port B rises, Pulse, port B falls. The two pulses never overlap, and the delay between them is programmable from 10 µs upwards. All three stimulation modes, Continuous, Burst and NPS, and both Manual and Trigger operation are preserved<br/>


## Main Modifications

- **User interface:** The Nextion touchscreen interface (Arduino Leonardo) was replaced with a 16×2 I2C LCD and rotary encoder (Arduino Uno), reducing power consumption and simplifying assembly.

- **Battery monitor:** The LM741/zener/LED battery-monitoring circuit was removed to reduce battery consumption.

- **Amplifiers:** TL072 op-amps were replaced with OPA2197 devices, providing rail-to-rail operation, low offset, and operation up to 36 V. This makes better use of the available supply voltage and increases the output compliance range.

- **Simplified signal chain:** The two inverting summing amplifiers (four Timer4 signals) were replaced by a single differential amplifier (A − B) followed by a buffer.

- **Current adjustment:** The resistor board was replaced by a 10-turn potentiometer with a dial and range selector (1 mA, 100 µA, and 10 µA), allowing direct current adjustment and readout.

- **Output protection:** A DC-blocking capacitor (CBLK) and bleeder resistor (RBLEED) were added to the output, together with pull-down resistors on the inputs. A digital input isolator, passive current limiter, and output-enable stage using a PhotoMOS relay or conventional relay are currently under evaluation.


## Specifications

Values below come from circuit simulation and are pending bench validation.

- **Output current:** Up to 1 mA, with selectable ranges of 1 mA, 100 µA, and 10 µA.

- **Maximum load at 1 mA:** Simulation with a ±18 V battery supply indicates approximately 17 kΩ with one 1N4007 diode per rail and 16 kΩ with two diodes per rail. A working load of up to 12 kΩ is recommended.

- **Compliance voltage:** Approximately 17 V with a ±18 V battery supply. The passive current limiter currently under evaluation would reduce this by approximately 2 V.

- **Battery-dependent compliance:** Maximum load follows approximately Load_max = Compliance / Current. At 1 mA, every 1 V decrease in compliance reduces the maximum load by approximately 1 kΩ. With each rail decreasing from approximately 19 V to 14 V over battery life, the corresponding maximum load is approximately 18 kΩ to 13 kΩ.

- **Frequency:** 0.10–500 Hz (firmware-limited).

- **Minimum phase duration:** 100 µs in Continuous and Burst modes; 50 µs in NPS mode (firmware-limited).

- **Maximum phase duration:** Firmware-limited, capped at 999.99 ms in all modes.
    - Biphasic: (Period − 100 µs − Delay) / 2
    - Monophasic: Period − 100 µs
    - NPS: ITImin replaces the period.
    - Examples (biphasic, 25 µs delay): approximately 9.9 ms at 50 Hz and 0.94 ms at 500 Hz.

- **Hardware limitation, per phase (both modes):** during a phase, the blocking capacitor charges by I × t / C. The phase must end before this voltage consumes the compliance margin of the current source. For example, at 1 mA into a 10 kΩ load (~7 V of margin) with a 47 µF CBLK, the maximum phase duration is about 330 ms. 

- **Hardware limitation, charge accumulation (monophasic only):** in monophasic mode all charge flows in the same direction and accumulates over the pulse train, and RBLEED is what keeps it bounded. The steady-state voltage across CBLK is approximately I × duty cycle × RBLEED, reached with a time constant of RBLEED × CBLK (~8 min with 10 MΩ and 47 µF). At 1 mA, 100 µs phases and 4 Hz, this settles around 4 V, which has to be subtracted from the available compliance. If I × duty cycle × RBLEED exceeds the compliance margin, the output saturates before steady state is reached; the train then has to end within about CBLK × margin / average current. In balanced biphasic mode this does not occur: the charge of each phase is returned by the next one, and only the mismatch between phases remains. For a 4 µs mismatch at 1 mA and 4 Hz, the residual is about 0.16 V.

- **Possible solution for long monophasic protocols (under evaluation):** a larger CBLK (e.g. 100 µF film) extends both limits while keeping DC protection. If the protocol requires true net charge, an optional jumper/switch footprint can short CBLK, making the output DC-coupled with no charge accumulation. This also removes the DC protection (op-amp offset and faults reach the tissue) and used together with the current limiter and output-enable stage.
